Saturday's 4h15m long ride was the week's headline: power faded 33% across the ride while HR climbed, about 30% aerobic decoupling, with a spiky VI of 1.18. That's a big gap between effort and output on the week's largest session. Monday's 15-mile long run, by contrast, was well controlled — pace held steady in the 10:30-11:00/mi band with only a 2.2% first-to-last fade and modest per-rep decoupling, despite the highest single-session load of the week. Thursday's sweet spot ride showed a mild 5% power fade across the intervals, nothing alarming. The brick run off Saturday's ride was short (7.8 min) but showed 9% decoupling and a pace swing to 14:14/mi mid-effort before recovering — consistent with running off a ride that had already faded hard.

TSB stayed deeply negative all week, from -46.2 on Monday recovering to -24.2 by Sunday, with a dip back to -38.9 after Saturday's long ride — still buried, though trending in the right direction by week's end. CTL is up to 84.8, +9.7 over the last six weeks, so the fitness is building. Worth flagging: bike eFTP sits at 142.5W against a set FTP of 160W, nearly 11% below — that gap could be inflating bike IF and load numbers, which may also be feeding into how dramatic Saturday's fade reading looks. Run eFTP (237.6) tracks closely with the set FTP (235), so those numbers look solid. VO2max held flat at 51 on both tests this week. Sleep and HRV softened toward the weekend — sleep scores fell to 49 and 46 on Saturday and Sunday (the week's worst nights), HRV dropped to 50 on Sunday from a 60-64 range all week, and resting HR ticked up to 57 from a 52-55 baseline. Those three moving together on the same day the swim got missed is worth keeping an eye on. One SpO2 reading of 91 on Tuesday is also worth a mention, though it's a single data point.
