01Volume & load · four weeks

Swim Bike Run bar height = training load · this week solid

15.2 hours and load 847, up from 13.8h/831 the week before, 12.1h/908, and 10.1h/748 before that. Hours have climbed three weeks straight; load is essentially flat against that 830-900 band, meaning more time went in without a matching rise in strain. Bike carried the week: 7.6 hours and load 400, both season highs, built around the 5h15 long ride, the single biggest session of the block. Run hours rose to 4.8h but load fell to 309 from 414 the week before, the lowest run load in four weeks even with an 18-mile long run in it - this week's run work leaned aerobic rather than intense. Swim held at 3 sessions, 2.8 hours, load 138, continuing a slow climb from 65 to 105 over the past month.

The 5h15 long ride was the standout: power eased from about 100W to 96W across the four quartiles while HR crept from 132 to 135, roughly 6% aerobic decoupling over five-plus hours - solid control at that duration. The brick run right after it told a different story: pace held near 11:00/mi through three quarters, then blew out to 16:33/mi in the last quarter, a 50% pace fade and 28% decoupling. The ride stayed clean, so this reads as legs giving out after the longest ride of the year, not a ride problem. The FTP retest was messy: power bounced 186W, 158W, 183W, 143W across reps, a variability index of 1.25 and a 23% fade rep to rep - hard to trust as a clean 20-minute number. The 18-mile long run was the week's cleanest aerobic work: pace tightened from 11:13/mi to the 10:40s in the back half while HR drifted from 140 to 148, essentially flat decoupling.

10 of 10 planned sessions completed, nothing missed, nothing moved, nothing unplanned - a fully executed week. Total load landed at 847 against 835 planned, basically matched at the top line, but that hides a split. Run and bike sessions ran hot against plan: the long run came in at load 196 vs 178 planned, the long ride at 268 vs 227, the FTP session at 75 vs 36. All three swims went the other way: race pace swim 40 vs 80 planned, endurance swim 45 vs 93 planned, open water swim 53 vs 73 planned - each roughly half its target load despite swimming close to or longer than planned time. On paper it's a clean week; underneath it, swim intensity missed its mark three times.

TSB swung from -29.2 Monday up to -10.6 Friday as early load cleared, then dropped to -31.8 after Saturday's long ride before easing to -22.9 Sunday - a normal sawtooth for a big-volume week in peak build. Sleep dipped to a low of 5.5h Thursday, then rebounded to 9.2h and 8.4h over the weekend, but sleep quality (inverted scale) got worse on those same high-hour nights, worth keeping an eye on since more time in bed didn't translate to better-rated sleep. CTL kept climbing, now 94.6 and up 25 points over six weeks, so the work is landing. VO2max slipped 51 to 50, likely just noise. eFTP for both sports now sits essentially at or just above set FTP - ride 142.7 vs 140 set, run 237.6 vs 235 set - fitness has caught up to the current targets, though the fragmented FTP test this week makes the ride number less trustworthy than usual.

05Questions for coach

All three swims landed well under planned load this week (40 vs 80, 45 vs 93, 53 vs 73) despite full time completion - is that pacing intentional, or worth a look at swim intensity targets?

The FTP retest was fragmented, with a 1.25 variability index and 23% power fade rep to rep - is that number usable for setting ride FTP, or does it need a cleaner retest?

The brick run cratered late with a 50% pace fade right after the season's longest ride - is that fade worth tracking as the long-ride-to-brick pairing gets longer, or expected at this stage?
