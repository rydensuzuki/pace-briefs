01Volume & load · four weeks

Swim Bike Run bar height = training load · this week solid

Total load was 682 across 11.6 hours and 9 sessions, down from 847/15.2h the week before and well below the 12-15h range of the prior three weeks. Every discipline pulled back in hours except swim, which held steady at 3.0h and actually carried the highest relative load share (170) it's had all month. Bike dropped from 7.6h to 5.5h and run from 4.8h to 3.1h, even though the long ride itself (3h30, load 183) was the single biggest session of the week. Planned load was 720, so at 682 actual you landed at 95% — this wasn't a week that fell apart, it was a smaller week by design, sitting right before a much bigger one on the calendar.

The Sunday long run (14mi, 2h20m) was clean: pace tightened from 10:43 to 10:36/mi across the set with decoupling never exceeding about 5%, even though it ran hotter than your last three long runs. Saturday's 3h30 long ride was the standout of the block — watts held flat across all four quartiles (98-101W), decoupling near zero, fade just 2.1%. That's exactly the kind of aerobic ride you want heading into a 6 hour week. On the swim side, Friday's 3200yd endurance swim showed stroke rate falling 36% from the first rep to the last (28.4 to 18.3 spm) while HR climbed into the 150s-160s — a real fatigue signature, not just pacing. Sunday's 4500yd open water swim showed a smaller but similar pattern, cadence fading 8.7% with 4.1% decoupling. Two swims in the same week losing stroke rate as the effort wore on is worth noting even though each individually wasn't extreme.

Form improved steadily across the week — TSB moved from -25.9 on Monday to -12.4 by Sunday, meaning fatigue (ATL) came down faster than fitness (CTL), which held essentially flat around 95. That's the picture of a week built to absorb the prior block before loading up again. Sleep dipped hard mid-week — 4.0 hours Tuesday night into Wednesday, the lowest of the whole block, on a night you still executed hill sprints well. It recovered from there, back to 6.5-7.7 hours the rest of the week. HRV tracked with it, dipping to 54 midweek and climbing back to 62-63 by the weekend; resting HR stayed steady in the low 50s throughout, which is a good sign the short sleep hit didn't linger. CTL is up 24.6 over the last 43 days, a real fitness gain. eFTP for the run (237) sits right at the set FTP (235), so run intensity targets look accurate. Ride eFTP (142.5) now sits slightly above the set FTP (140) for the first time in a while — a small gap, but if it holds or widens it means ride sessions may be a touch easier than the numbers suggest.

Ride eFTP has crept just above the set FTP of 140 — worth a small bump, or is it too marginal to matter yet?
